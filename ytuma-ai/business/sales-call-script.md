# Discovery / Sales Call Script

*(Cleaned up from the source draft — same structure and rapport-building approach, generic language,
no discount-until-desperate energy.)*

## 1. Open — get them talking

"Before we get into the details, walk me through your current process the way you'd explain it to someone
new. What actually happens, step by step, from [first contact] to [job closed]?"

Let them talk. Don't interrupt.

## 2. Name the pain

"So you're doing [X, Y, Z] manually, every time — and each one is eating time you could be spending on
the parts of the job that actually make money. Is that a fair summary?"

Get a yes. That's the anchor you'll refer back to in the pitch.

## 3. Introduce the solution — plainly

"What I build is an AI assistant that handles the repetitive parts — follow-ups, drafting replies,
scheduling, pulling files — from something as simple as a text message or a phone call.

You ask for something in plain language. It does it, or it asks you to approve it first if it's something
that goes out to a client."

## 4. Make it concrete

"For example: instead of you spending 20 minutes writing a follow-up email, you say 'draft a follow-up to
[client] about [thing],' review what it wrote in about 10 seconds, and approve it. Nothing goes out without
your say-so unless you decide later you want it fully automatic."

## 5. Differentiate from "just another automation"

"The part that matters is what happens when something breaks — because everything breaks eventually. This
system retries things intelligently, and if something fails repeatedly, it tells you immediately instead of
silently dropping a client's email. Most no-code automations don't have that. This does."

## 6. Walk the process

"Here's how it goes:
1. We map your current workflow
2. You share your templates, past examples, and pricing info
3. I build and connect the assistant to your email/calendar/phone
4. We test it together
5. You approve it
6. It goes live"

## 7. Price with confidence — no hedging

"Pricing depends on scope. [Starter tier] covers email and basic automation. [Standard tier] — which is
where most people land — covers full follow-up automation, email drafting, memory, and a messaging
assistant. [Pro tier] adds phone agents and a full monitoring/observability layer.

Based on what you've described, you're likely in the [tier] range."

State the number. Don't apologize for it, don't immediately offer a discount.

## 8. Set a realistic timeline

"Once I have your materials, it's typically [X] weeks to full delivery, plus testing time before go-live."

## 9. Close

"By the end of this, you'll have an assistant that remembers your clients, drafts your communications, and
tells you the moment anything needs your attention — instead of you carrying all of that in your head.

Do you want to start with the [Starter] version, or go straight to [Standard] since that's closer to what
you actually described?"

Give a binary choice, not an open-ended "so what do you think."

## Objection notes

- **"How is this different from ChatGPT?"** — "ChatGPT doesn't have access to your calendar, your CRM, or
  your inbox, and it doesn't remember your last client conversation. This does, and it's built specifically
  around your process, not a generic chat window."
- **"What if it makes a mistake?"** — "Anything that goes out to a client — an email, for example — goes
  through you for approval by default. You control what's automatic and what isn't."
- **"What happens if it breaks?"** — This is your strongest answer: point to the retry/error-handling
  system directly. "It's built to notice its own failures and tell me immediately, not fail silently."
